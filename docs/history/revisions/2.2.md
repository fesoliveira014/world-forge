# v2.2 — Lowlands, forest climate, and river hierarchy

The observed problems were widespread high terrain, cold land climates, restrictive forest rainfall cutoffs, and an annual river overlay that gave small cold-region channels too much visual weight. This revision keeps the existing pipeline, controls, UI, and receiver graph.

## Changes

- **Local relief.** The continent field controls coastlines and gentle inland gradients. A saturating lowland profile replaces broad high continental relief. Narrow Gaussian collision and arc envelopes produce localized ranges; small-scale mountain texture follows those envelopes. The relief control scales plains and mountains without independently moving the sea datum.
- **Climate at the displayed elevation.** The seasonal temperature baseline has warmer middle latitudes and stronger continental summer/winter variation. Each fine cell receives a lapse-rate correction relative to its process-grid height; evaporation demand uses that corrected temperature. The temperature and rainfall layers now show the same fine fields used by the inspector and biome classifier.
- **Forest eligibility.** Cool humid forests use water relative to evaporative demand instead of a high fixed rainfall requirement. Tropical forest requires year-round warmth; rainforest also requires a wet dry season. The default ocean evaporation multiplier rises from 1.2 to 1.5. No latitude-based forest painting or biome-area quotas are used.
- **Connected interior drainage.** Flatter terrain exposed several perched depressions inside otherwise closed lake patches. Only affected patches are refined into downhill catchments, balanced at their connecting saddles. Each pond either closes locally or supplies its downstream lake. The original corner graph remains authoritative.
- **Readable river hierarchy.** Small tributaries are narrower; major channels retain prominence. Frozen channels become grey and lose their moving pulses in seasonal views. Surface segments frozen in all four samples are hidden. Discharge and width are still annual means, and annual mode remains an annual network view.

Warmer temperatures also increase evaporative demand, so desert area does not fall everywhere. Forest totals and broad relief remain comparable when surface resolution rises. The remaining tropical tundra averages about 3,950 m elevation: it represents alpine terrain rather than cold equatorial lowlands. Drainage density remains uneven with precipitation and contributing area; increasing resolution adds smaller catchments rather than preserving a fixed channel count.

The default longest connected channel above 100 m³/s is about 4,092 km. All measured channel edges descend or remain level when lake surface elevation is included. The final default's global water ledger residual is below 10⁻⁸ m³/s, and summed individual lake transfers agree with the global exchange. The nested-pond fixture checks a perched spilling pond feeding a closed lower lake. TypeScript, four focused core tests, and the production build are the validation gates. No browser visual QA or broad seed sweep was performed.

These observations are not targets for every planet or a claim of Earth climate accuracy. Four representative seasons cannot reproduce monthly freeze/thaw events. A warm-season river is appropriate in polar regions; this model does not simulate glacier-surface drainage or subglacial flow. Numerical data and configurations are in [revision-2.2-measurements.json](../../observations/history/revision-2.2-measurements.json).

## Reference interpretation

- [NASA's world topography](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/topography-of-the-world-3741/) and [South America topography](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/topography-of-south-america-3581/) informed the contrast between broad lowlands and localized mountains. Elevation colors and shaded slopes are a visual reference, not a histogram to fit.
- [Met Office climate zones](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/climate/climate-explained/climate-zones) describes temperature and precipitation criteria; [NASA's tundra overview](https://science.nasa.gov/kids/earth/mission-biomes/biotundra/) distinguishes Arctic and high-mountain tundra. The game's thresholds approximate these relationships with four seasonal samples.
- [NOAA's Arctic river discharge report](https://arctic.noaa.gov/report-card/report-card-2021/river-discharge/) documents real Arctic river flow and strong seasonal snowmelt influence. [USGS snowmelt runoff](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/snowmelt-runoff-and-water-cycle) supports retaining thaw-fed rivers rather than excluding all polar channels by latitude.
