# v2.7.0 — globe, flat map, and coastlines

This revision changes presentation. Generator identity remains **2.6.0** and the generated fields and project JSON format are unchanged.

## Observable behavior

- Choose **Globe** or **Flat map** above the active layer title. Switching keeps the world, active layer, season, and selected cell, and fits the new layout to the viewport.
- The map has north up and east right. Drag with the mouse or one finger to pan; scroll, pinch, or use the zoom buttons to zoom. **Reset view** fits the whole map. Panning stays within the map's visible extent.
- All 23 fields, seasonal colors, cell/plate boundaries, river ribbons, bank vegetation, and animated wind/current trails work on the map. The natural view retains geographic procedural pigment rather than applying screen-space texture.
- **Coastlines** draws present-day land/ocean boundaries over any field in either layout. It is independent of rivers and plate borders, and off by default. The initial-continents layer can therefore be compared with the present-day coast. Lake shores are not included in landmass outlines.
- Globe rotation, exaggerated 3D terrain, and the display-only plate drift preview remain globe features. Changing layout resets that drift preview.

## Projection and mesh handling

For a spherical position `(x, y, z)`, use `longitude = atan2(z, x)` and `latitude = atan2(y, hypot(x, z))`. The plane spans `[-π, π] × [-π/2, π/2]`. An orthographic camera keeps it flat; camera zoom changes presentation only. This is the [equirectangular / Plate Carrée projection](https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/eqc.html). It is easy to navigate and compare latitude bands, but enlarges high-latitude regions. Use spherical cell areas for numerical comparisons.

`projection.ts` transforms the existing indexed geometry. Ordinary vertices remain shared. Triangles crossing ±180° are locally unwrapped, duplicated at the opposite edge, and clipped to the rectangular frame in their material. This avoids triangles spanning the map. At an exact pole, a sector becomes a quad with a top/bottom edge. If grid jitter places the pole inside a triangle, the polar value is interpolated from the three vertices, and the triangle is divided into three sectors.

Source vertices retain their indices. Added seam/pole vertices store their source indices and interpolation weights. Palette and seasonal changes refresh those extra attributes after updating the original data. Categorical dual fans keep each cell's color. Natural shading uses retained spherical positions for noise and flat-facing normals for illumination. Analytic palettes remain unlit.

Picking intersects the map plane, rejects clicks outside the rectangle, then applies the inverse:

```ts
x = cos(latitude) * cos(longitude);
y = sin(latitude);
z = cos(latitude) * sin(longitude);
```

The existing spherical nearest-cell walk identifies the cell. The highlighted dual-cell outline uses the same projection and seam splitting as other line overlays.

## Flow and shoreline overlays

Coastline edges are exactly those whose adjacent surface cells have different ocean-mask values. The ocean-connectivity field is the authority, not a new elevation cutoff. Outlines follow the shared dual-cell edges. Natural shading interpolates its land mask, so its visual coastline can differ slightly from this cell boundary. The coastline group has its own lifecycle; toggling it does not rebuild river ribbons or particle pools.

Wind/current tracers still advect on the sphere through the existing seasonal field, including the ocean blocking rules. Only their displayed segments are projected. Each segment has room for two pieces: a crossing segment ends at one map edge and resumes at the other. A non-crossing segment uses a degenerate second piece. Respawn and animation semantics are unchanged.

River and bank meshes pass through the same static mesh projection. Their existing downstream-distance UV coordinates and temperature/activity attributes survive seam duplication, so animated pulses retain their phase through confluences and across the map cut.

## Scope and cost

No map texture, extra climate pass, new spatial index, or second simulation snapshot is introduced. A layout change replaces its display geometry and camera; it does not retain both layouts’ GPU resources. Projection construction is linear in the displayed mesh, with extra vertices only at the seam and poles. Flow output uses up to twice the line vertices in map mode; tracer count, history length, and spherical lookup work remain bounded as before. Categorical views at maximum resolution still have a substantial memory cost.

The current implementation uses one rectangular projection and a fixed central meridian. Map boundaries are clipped, not an infinitely tiled world. Display settings are session-local, as before.

## Focused verification

Three small tests cover forward/inverse coordinates and cell picking, complete projected area at the seam and poles on regular and jittered grids, field propagation to added vertices, and dateline flow segments in both directions. The existing shallow-water rendering regression also remains relevant. TypeScript and the production build validate integration; no browser or visual QA was requested.

These are suggestions, not implemented modes. The river-system highlight is particularly useful before settlement/history simulation, because it exposes connectivity already present in the model.
