# v2.12.0 — Shared lake state and conservative rainfall

Application and generator version **2.12.0** implement the four priority recommendations from the AETHER-0004 generation review. Reload the application and regenerate saved projects. All existing v2 recipes remain importable. The new fields change generated water, habitats, and fertility; saved recipes are not frozen snapshots.

## What changes in the world

- Seasonal lake coverage is shared by ecology, fertility, the inspector, and all three rendering styles in both layouts. Mixed shore cells retain their exposed land. Only fully wet cells have no land habitat.
- Lakes receive liquid rain plus released snowmelt from the existing snow budget. Four representative quarters track storage, evaporation and spill.
- Fine-grid rainfall varies within coarse climate cells while preserving each parent's rainfall volume and nonnegativity.
- Accessible lake shores receive a freshwater-suitability contribution based on throughflow relative to evaporation. Closed basins remain uncertain; incoming river corridors retain their own freshwater contribution.

The continent system, mineral presets and existing river display curves remain. The optional proposal to migrate one channel across several routing reaches is deferred: it would also require coherent cutoff placement and terrain migration. This release adds no new generation controls or additional climate solves.

## One fractional lake state

`lake-state.ts` owns `lakeCoverageAt`, `exposedLandFraction`, dominant reservoir lookup, and the freshwater proxy. `lakeCoverage` is the mean of four season-end coverage fields; a selected quarter uses its own field. Ocean cells have no exposed land. A land cell's usable fraction is `1 − lakeCoverage`.

The drainage graph still uses a maximum wet footprint and real spill links. This gives perennial graph connectivity without rebuilding the river network every quarter. `lake-surfaces.ts` separately rasterizes each lake's **recorded seasonal area** from low samples upward. Equal-height samples share the remaining fraction of their group. Each corner's share is distributed to its incident cells using the same area weights as the reservoir calculation.

The distinction matters on flat sills: reconstructing a footprint with a second strict water-level comparison can gain or lose an entire terrace because of floating-point rounding. Using the recorded area keeps the reservoir, coverage and suitability ledgers consistent. The fractional footprint is a regional area representation, not an exact subcell shoreline.

`classifyEcology` keeps the regional land biome wherever land remains. Wetland and floodplain fractions are capped by exposed area. Seasonal emergence contributes local wetland potential where warmth and gentle slopes permit it. Fertility computes the regional and corridor potentials on that land, then forms an area-weighted cell score; permanent water contributes zero. A mixed cell can have an accessible shoreline even without a fully wet neighbor.

Natural, Shaded relief and Ink atlas blend seasonal inland coverage on the existing surface. Ocean coasts retain their crisp boundary. Water/habitat diagnostic palettes use the same fractional coverage; the fertility palette shows the already area-weighted score directly. There is no separate lake triangle mesh. River mouths use coverage-weighted incident lake anchors. The terrain mesh retains its routing high-water height while seasonal appearance changes.

## Seasonal reservoir calculation

`solveLandWater` already stores snowfall and releases bounded temperature-dependent melt. It now retains `seasonalLiquid` (rain plus actual melt, mm per quarter) and `seasonalPet`. Frozen precipitation does not bypass that storage when it falls on the provisional lake footprint. Permanent cold can continue accumulating snow; this is not a glacier mass-balance model.

The annual sorted height/area calculation initializes a lake. `seasonal-reservoir.ts` builds cumulative area, bed-volume and quarter-specific liquid precipitation, evaporation demand, and displaced provisional runoff along that same height curve. A binary search obtains the wet area for a given liquid store.

Each quarter has four equal substeps. For a substep of duration `dt`:

```text
available = storage + inflow × dt + wetLiquidPrecipitation × dt
            − displacedLandRunoff × dt
evaporated = min(available, wetPET × dt)
spill = max(0, available − evaporated − capacity)
newStorage = clamp(available − evaporated − spill, 0, capacity)
```

Displaced runoff is the footprint's provisional land runoff, capped by available inflow. It is removed because lake precipitation/evaporation replace that same land bucket. Spill is a transfer of existing water. Frozen lake tint does not block liquid arriving from upstream.

A bracketed search, bounded at 32 annual cycles, initializes storage without simulating centuries for deep lakes. It stops near a repeating year where the discretized curve permits one. The model always retains `storageChange`; a perfectly repeating initial cycle is not guaranteed on stepped height curves. In the final AETHER-0004 run, maximum remaining annual storage drift was equivalent to 10.92 m³/s in one lake; the signed total was −10.06 m³/s. The dry check's maximum was 22.86 m³/s. These are explicit stock changes, not lost water or a numerical budget residual.

`Lake.seasonal` records four season-end stocks, levels and areas, plus quarter-mean inflow, liquid precipitation, evaporation, displaced runoff and outflow. Annual scalar fluxes are their means. The exact quarter ledger is:

```text
inflow + liquidPrecipitation − displacedRunoff − evaporation − outflow
    = storageChange / quarterDuration
```

Spilling lakes use their sill for the routing high-water level; closed lakes use the highest sampled seasonal level. The nested-basin merge tree retains its bounded local refinement. Incremental nested spill follows the emitting lake's seasonal pattern; it is not a fully coupled transient depression hierarchy. River virtual nodes emit the recorded quarterly lake releases instead of the old residence-time attenuation formula. The existing final normalization preserves annual corner means in Float32 seasonal fields.

## Rainfall transfer

`operators/prolongation.ts` first calculates each parent's mapped child area and area-weighted child-position centroid. For each season it forms:

```text
mean[j] = coarseRain[j] × coarseArea[j] / mappedChildArea[j]
delta[i] = dot(coarseGradient[j], position[i] − childCentroid[j])
fineRain[i] = mean[j] + limiter[j] × delta[i]
```

The centroid gives a zero area-weighted sum of deviations inside each parent. A single limiter per parent keeps every child between neighboring coarse extrema and at or above zero. Thus a parent retains its rain volume without another rescaling that would recreate constant patches. Float32 storage introduces only rounding-scale error.

This reconstruction reduces process-cell outlines; it does not add a finer climate simulation or erase coarse rain shadows. Fine terrain still adjusts temperature and PET independently.

## Freshwater suitability

For a lake with water area, the prototype proxy is:

```text
flushing = outflow / max(tiny, outflow + evaporation)
freshwaterSuitability = 0.2 + 0.8 × sqrt(clamp(flushing, 0, 1))
```

A dry basin contributes zero. A closed basin retains a cautious nonzero potential and is labeled **Freshwater uncertain · closed basin**. Low flushing is labeled as concentration risk; substantial throughflow gives freshwater potential. These are uncalibrated suitability weights, not salinity or drinking-water estimates. Lake size does not determine freshwater. Nearby shore contributions are coverage weighted, and the strongest independent river contribution remains available.

Maximum relative rain-volume error per parent was `8.06 × 10⁻⁸`. Cell-weighted lake area and the reservoir objects differed by 0.123 km² over 10.927 million km², consistent with Float32 coverage rounding. About 244,562 km² lies within the difference between maximum and minimum seasonal wet extent; this is not a simultaneous flood area. There are 174 reservoirs whose sampled wet area varies by more than 1 km².

The corresponding northern reservoir near the review's lake #464 is #459 in this regeneration. Its direct liquid precipitation falls from approximately 9,175 to 750 m³/s and outflow from 9,098 to 404 m³/s. This is the combined regenerated result, including rainfall reconstruction and terrain/water feedback; it is not an isolated snow-only experiment. Cells frozen in all four seasons contribute zero direct liquid precipitation. IDs are snapshot-local.

The full run took 203.8 seconds, including about 158.1 seconds in climate. Baseline timing was 219.5 seconds in a separate run; these are environment-specific observations, not a speedup claim. The new retained fields add about 31.25 MB. JS lake objects, temporary arrays and GPU allocations are excluded from these byte counts.

The contrasting `AETHER-DRY-CHECK` used 40,962 surface cells, 2,562 process cells, warmer forcing, reduced moisture supply/recycling and 55% target ocean. It retained approximately 49.39% desert and 3.07% lake water across non-ocean area. Its 434 lakes include 287 closed basins. This is one stress case, not a climate calibration sweep.

Exact effective configurations and measured summaries are in [generation-2.12.0.json](../../observations/generation-2.12.0.json). The original AETHER-0004 configuration remains under `examples/`; the contrasting recipe is [AETHER-DRY-CHECK.json](../../../examples/AETHER-DRY-CHECK.json).

## Verification and limits

All 33 focused tests pass. Added checks cover conservative nonnegative rainfall, snow-to-liquid timing, quarterly reservoir budgets, seasonal coverage area, and exposed-shore fertility. TypeScript and the production build pass. The changed simulation modules pass their lint rules; existing React hook lint findings in `WorldView.tsx` remain outside this hydrology change.

Offscreen OpenGL scene checks exercised Natural, Shaded relief and Ink atlas in both Globe and Flat map on a reduced 10,242-cell AETHER-0004 seed world. This checked the actual shader/mesh path, not an interactive browser session or the full-resolution reference scene. Six images are retained under `observations/seasonal-water/`.

Annual drainage residual was `1.82 × 10⁻¹⁵` relative in AETHER-0004 and `1.02 × 10⁻¹⁴` in the dry case. Maximum quarter-lake budget residual was below `2.4 × 10⁻⁸ m³/s`. These numerical checks establish accounting consistency, not predictive realism.

The model remains four representative seasons with substeps, a fixed annual routing graph, bounded initialization, coarse basin hypsometry and a freshwater proxy. It does not simulate salt transport, daily floods, a coupled ice sheet, lake evaporation feedback to the atmosphere, or multi-reach channel migration. No continent rewrite or mineral-system expansion was needed for these improvements.
