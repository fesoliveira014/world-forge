# Atlas 2.24.0 — grayscale heightmap

The new **Heightmap** entry in Map style maps the world's minimum terrain
elevation to black and its maximum to white. Intermediate elevations interpolate
linearly through gray; a halfway elevation displays as 128/255. The legend shows
the current world's actual low and high elevations in metres.

The shader reads the existing packed 16-bit terrain elevation and decodes it
before interpolation. Its source encoding range can be wider than the actual
world range; only the latter controls black and white. Ocean floors and lakebeds
are included. Lake water surfaces, seasonal snow, biome color and riverbank
pigment do not replace the underlying elevation.

The globe uses a smooth sphere, and the flat map uses its geographic projection
without relief displacement. Lighting, tone mapping and color-space conversion
do not alter grayscale brightness. A constant-height world displays neutral
gray. The style uses the existing shared terrain material and batching path,
without new per-vertex attributes, textures or saved-world data.

Colored overlays and selection marks are hidden in Heightmap. The footer states
that the view shows elevation only, and unrelated display controls are hidden.
The user's overlay and relief settings return when another style is selected.
Geographic cell inspection remains available in both projections.

Existing worlds work without regeneration. Application version is 2.24.0;
generator identity remains 2.17.1. Saving remains manual.

## Verification

Focused tests cover source-range decoding, actual-world normalization,
black/white endpoints, midpoint brightness, constant or invalid ranges,
projection clipping, flat geometry and shared-material integration.

All 442 tests and whole-project TypeScript pass. Browser checks use the preserved
251,559-cell AETHER-2408 world, whose terrain spans −7,440.667 m to 12,564.306 m.
Both projections produce exact grayscale pixels. Changing the season, relief
or overlay preferences leaves the heightmap unchanged, and style/camera round
trips reproduce the same pixels. Polar and dateline views have no WebGL errors.

A separate GPU check renders black, middle gray and white as exactly 0, 128 and
255 in both material variants, even with bright colored lighting and renderer
tone mapping enabled. Constant-height terrain renders 128 throughout.

Native UI checks verify the menu, grayscale legend, rounded elevation limits
and restoration of the previous overlay settings. A held river request confirms
that Heightmap is usable once terrain finishes, without waiting for hidden
rivers or showing their loading banner. All 22 browser checks pass.
