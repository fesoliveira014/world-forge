# v2.2.2 — River shader and natural surface

The river material introduced `active` as a GLSL varying. That identifier is reserved in GLSL ES, which caused compilation to fail and prevented the entire river mesh from rendering. It is now named `vFlowActivity`; seasonal freezing and downstream pulses are preserved. The river shader also uses three.js output color conversion.

Natural world previously interpolated biome colors over the simulation mesh under strong Phong lighting. Its new material evaluates fine surface texture per fragment, keeping weather resolution and generation time independent of visual grain. It uses:

- Continuous vegetation potential and interpolated forest cover for land colors.
- Seeded seamless 3D grain, with details smaller than a pixel faded to avoid shimmer.
- Rock pigment and slight normal detail concentrated at high elevations.
- Ocean depth colors, distinct inland water, restrained glints, and a sharp antialiased land/water transition.
- Local temperature, permanent ice, and snow storage for seasonal cover.

The detailed shader changes appearance only. It does not invent terrain geometry, move river receivers, or perturb coastlines with noise. The finest geographic features still depend on surface-grid resolution. Other data overlays keep their existing mesh, colors, and controls. Two vec4 attributes cost 32 additional bytes per surface vertex, approximately 20 MiB at maximum surface detail.

Verification was limited to reproducing the old reserved-word failure and compiling/linking the corrected river and natural shaders with Khronos glslang using GLSL ES 3.00 and three.js shader chunks, followed by TypeScript and the production build. Browser visual QA was not performed. Core simulation and generator identity remain v2.2.1; the five existing simulation tests were not rerun for this rendering-only change.

[GLSL ES 3.00 specification, reserved keywords](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/specs/es/3.0/GLSL_ES_Specification_3.00.pdf) documents why the original shader was invalid. The surface shader uses the existing three.js APIs and introduces no dependency or external texture download.
