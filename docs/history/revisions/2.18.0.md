# Atlas 2.18 — shared terrain and river resources

The fixed-detail viewer was retaining identical terrain fields in separate map
arrays and keeping riverbank inputs after painting the bank texture. This release
reduces those retained allocations while preserving every physical terrain face,
water sample and rendering order.

## Changes

- Map batches that need no seam or pole expansion share unchanged attributes and
  indices with their immutable source packets. Projected positions remain separate.
  Seam and pole expansion retains its existing interpolation and ownership rules.
- All terrain batches in the active appearance share one material. The view owns
  its lifetime and updates common uniforms once; disposing a batch releases only
  its geometry. The opaque sort preserves their previous packet order, preventing
  depth-tie differences around distant terrain edges. Other materials retain
  Three's default ordering, and transparent ordering is unchanged.
- One river asset owns the compact water packets and bank atlas across projection
  scene recreation. Bank-only packets and bank color/width arrays are released
  after painting. Water positions, flow phase, activity, width limits, continuation
  guides and source data keep their original values and order.
- Once the entire asset is retained, the viewer releases its idle preparation
  worker, including the original bank inputs, draping lookup arrays and page cache.
  A stale completion cannot release a different season or threshold. Terrain,
  inspection and overlay readers continue independently.

This is a display-memory update. Application version is 2.18.0; generator identity
remains 2.17.0. Existing saved worlds can be opened without regeneration. Saving
remains manual. No new disk cache, camera-dependent detail, spatial batching,
multi-draw, horizon culling or numeric quantization is introduced.

## Verification

All **287 automated tests** pass, including buffer aliasing and source immutability,
exact spherical picking from projected triangles, shared-material disposal,
river-field/geometry parity, byte-identical atlas painting, asset reuse, stale
worker-release guards, and stable opaque terrain ordering. Typecheck, formatting
and production build pass.

An additional 40,962-cell comparison against 2.17.0 produced byte-identical terrain
positions, attributes, indices and bounds in all three styles. Its 20 batches
included 12 ordinary batches able to share their original fields, reducing
accounted CPU geometry storage by 1,451,804 bytes.

Display storage falls by **306.5 MiB (29.3%)**. This accounts for retained typed
arrays and estimated GPU storage; it excludes worker storage, browser/driver
overhead, material objects and temporary allocation peaks. The independent
unique-buffer audit also includes the hidden placeholder's 29,472 CPU and 29,472
estimated GPU bytes, which the renderer display counter omits. GPU geometry and
the bank atlas retain their full sizes. Worker termination releases additional
preparation storage outside this display total; no process-memory figure is claimed.

The bank atlas SHA-256 is byte-identical before and after. All 5,242,880 physical
terrain faces and 1,386,698 drawn river segments remain. The 94 fewer draw calls
come from removing empty bank-only meshes after atlas painting; there is no
spatial batching or new culling. Rendering the complete map remains expensive on
this software GPU; this release targets retained memory rather than promising a
frame-rate improvement.

The matched global map screenshots are **pixel-identical** (zero changed pixels).
Switching the loaded maximum world from map to globe retains the same river asset,
texture object and atlas pixel buffer, keeps the reader count at two, and leaves
data requests at 1,281 terrain requests plus 181 river requests, with zero calls
to camera sampling. The globe retains all 9,395,561 submitted triangles and uses
768,381,311 accounted display bytes, down from 940,698,343 bytes (**18.3%**).

The maximum-world map → globe → map roundtrip retains the exact asset, atlas and
pixel buffer without more data reads; its returned map is pixel-identical. The
matched close globe is also pixel-identical. The preserved opaque packet order
eliminates the distant globe edge differences initially caused by material sharing.

A fresh final-code 10,242-cell browser run passes all 11 lifecycle checks: projection
and style reuse, empty consumed-input queues, threshold replacement, rapid season
changes, source replacement, no camera sampling, worker release, and complete
asset/atlas disposal on view unmount. Ten screenshot comparisons cover all three
styles with rivers on/off, June/December activity, source colors and the north pole
at 26× relief. All ten images are pixel-identical to the baseline. No browser or
shader errors occurred.
