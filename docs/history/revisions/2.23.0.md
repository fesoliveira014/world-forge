# Atlas 2.23.0 — fantasy cartography

The Map style menu gains three styles for the Natural world layer:

- **Classic parchment:** warm ivory paper, sepia outlines and hatched mountain
  ranges, muted vegetation and water colors.
- **Engraved explorer:** clean cream paper, fine dark ridge drawings, detailed
  hatching and restrained color washes.
- **Painted fantasy atlas:** watercolor-like land and ocean pigments, painted
  mountain shadows, snowy summits and richer green woodland.

The new styles show geography on a smooth globe or flat map. Mountains remain
discernible through illustrations drawn from the saved terrain. Selecting a
diagnostic layer restores that layer's terrain relief; returning to Natural or
Shaded relief restores the user's exaggeration setting. The world and original
inspection values stay unchanged. Rivers, lake coverage and seasonal land colors
continue to use the saved simulation data.

## Rendering

Mountain ranges follow a temporary, bounded one-degree support field sampled
directly from saved terrain triangles. Bilateral height drops identify ridges;
connected high ground guides their paths. Subcell crest estimates and supported
path smoothing remove the sampling grid from the drawings. Peaks overlap along
these paths using the measured width of their visible artwork. Lower shoulders
add occasional smaller peaks or hills, while isolated summits must be actual
local maxima. Water and low valleys separate ranges.

Woodland illustrations use saved forest class and vegetation, with bounded
geographic bins and exclusion around mountain drawings. Latitude and elevation
choose stable tree variants. Panning, orbiting and zooming do not rebuild either
placement. Refining the underlying world can change the selected ridges or
woodland locations, but does not multiply illustration density with every new
triangle. The support field is only placement metadata; it never replaces the
terrain geometry or its fields.

Illustration density is fixed at continental scale. Very narrow ridges may have
no drawing; close zoom enlarges the existing artwork without introducing new
peaks. Some repeated summit shapes remain because the styles share a small
procedural glyph vocabulary.

A conservative water mask protects the complete illustration footprint around
oceans, lakes and partially submerged cells, including the dateline and poles.
Coastal symbols shrink or are omitted when there is insufficient dry land.
This deliberately leaves small islands and narrow strips of land less decorated.

Each active fantasy style owns one 1024-square procedural symbol atlas and one
instanced draw. Temporary placement fields are released after preparation;
source terrain packets are not retained by the illustration layer. Symbols follow
the globe surface, clip at map edges and fade when too small to read. Mountain
drawings keep their geographic size at close zoom so connected ranges cannot
pull apart; woodland drawings cap their display size during extreme closeups.
Northern peaks are drawn before southern foreground peaks. Soft, open foothills
blend the artwork into the land wash. No new disk cache or terrain resolution is
introduced.

Completed placements use 49 bytes per symbol in a private, immutable buffer set
beside the existing terrain packet cache. Style and projection changes reuse
them; only the active style's atlas, material and projected geometry are owned by
the view. A source, world snapshot, layer or season change replaces the cache.
Partial preparations are never published for reuse. This avoids repeating the
terrain scan when trying different art styles without retaining the larger
temporary ridge field or extra atlases.

The terrain, river ribbons, coastline and cell overlays all use the same flat
display geometry in these styles. Rivers gain matching ink/water palettes and
restrained animation while sharing the existing river asset and bank atlas.
Diagnostic layers retain their ordinary color and height behavior. The previous
Natural, Shaded relief and Ink atlas styles remain available.

## Verification

- All 427 tests pass, alongside TypeScript and changed-file formatting checks.
- Ridge tests cover curved ranges, parallel ranges separated by deep valleys,
  flat plateaus, monotone slopes, isolated summits, repeated summits along one
  range, packet-order independence, immutable input, refinement, the dateline
  and polar terrain. Symbol tests cover water clearance, woodland eligibility,
  seasonal identity, seam copies, flat globe anchors and resource disposal.
- Placement-cache tests verify exact Float64 seam decisions, private ownership,
  all styles and projections without a collector rerun, disposal, empty results,
  and exclusion of partial or failed preparation.
- All 18 final browser views pass: three styles, globe and flat map, and mountain,
  forest and genuinely refined lake scenes. Each view uses one instanced symbol
  draw. The actual Map style dropdown was exercised for all three presets.
- With animation paused, returning to the same camera after rotation and zoom
  reproduces identical pixels, symbol positions and atlas hashes. Polar and dateline views remain
  finite and hole-free. Camera changes do not request new terrain or river data.
- Fantasy relief settings 1 and 26 produce identical images in all six
  style/projection combinations. The globe stays at unit radius, the map at
  zero terrain height, and peak inspection still returns the saved 9,484.85 m
  physical elevation. Returning to Natural restores safe terrain clearance.
- The grid toggle reuses its existing data. Eighteen categorical comparisons
  remain pixel-identical to version 2.22.0 with Natural styling; tectonics stays
  flat while drainage and biomes keep their requested terrain relief.

Temporary placement fields occupy 3.03 MiB regardless of physical cell count and
are released after preparing the illustration asset. The 2,621,442-cell world
produced 359 symbols using about 9.36 MiB of illustration storage, plus 17.2 KiB
for reusable placements. Initial illustration preparation took about 19.6 s;
reusing placements reduced that part of a style switch to 5.8 ms, with no new
terrain or river reads. Cache comparisons reproduced the accepted images exactly.

Opening that world took 91.3 s and switching from Painted to Engraved took
30.8 s in the cloud's SwiftShader software renderer. Terrain rebuilding and
software graphics still dominate the switch; placement reuse does not make it
instantaneous. These are observations of the updated application, not a
before/after benchmark or a prediction for native GPUs. The complete browser
checks, resource counts and measurement limitations are recorded in
[the verification report](../../observations/measurements/fantasy-styles-browser-2.23.0.json).

## Design references

The user selected [Here Dragons Abound](https://heredragonsabound.blogspot.com/)
as a design and procedural-cartography resource. The following posts informed
the original artwork and geographic illustration approach:

- [Lord of the Rings Map Style](https://heredragonsabound.blogspot.com/2018/10/lord-of-rings-map-style.html):
  flat ink cartography, mountain/hill hierarchy and forest placement independent
  of the terrain triangulation.
- [New Mountain Style, Part 5](https://heredragonsabound.blogspot.com/2019/12/new-mountain-style-part-5.html):
  connected ridge drawings rather than isolated peak stamps.
- [New Mountain Style, Part 7](https://heredragonsabound.blogspot.com/2020/01/new-mountain-style-part-7.html):
  mountain-region centerlines, longer coherent chains and sparse surrounding
  peaks instead of parallel rows.
- [The Incredible Shrinking Mountain](https://heredragonsabound.blogspot.com/2016/12/the-incredible-shrinking-mountain.html):
  readable outlines and restrained hatching at smaller display scales.
- [Continent Maps, Part 2: Getting Consistent Views](https://heredragonsabound.blogspot.com/2018/10/continent-maps-part-2-getting.html):
  stable geographic features when the viewport changes.

These are references, not bundled artwork or code. The new symbol atlases are
drawn procedurally by Atlas. No remote art service is needed to view a world.

Application version is 2.23.0. Generator identity remains 2.17.1; existing worlds
work without regeneration. Saving remains manual.
