# Atlas 2.18.2 — lake and river continuity after refinement

Regional refinement could break a broad inland lake into cellular patches and
turn a smooth river into repeated sharp bends with pinched outlines. The fixes
address terrain transfer, inherited river curves and display interpolation on
unequal refined reaches.

## Terrain surface and lake floors

The old remap interpolated bedrock as point samples, transferred sediment as
conserved cell averages, then added them to reconstruct elevation. Those two
operators disagree when sediment varies, even when the original total surface
is exactly flat. The discrepancy introduced artificial bumps and pits before
drainage was rebuilt.

The remap now interpolates the existing total elevation and reconciles the
bedrock interface underneath the conserved sediment. Sediment, accumulated
incision and deposition still retain their area integrals. Total height remains
authoritative rather than being resummed from two rounded material fields.
Retained terrain samples and genuine raised islands survive refinement; no new
geological time is applied. This also covers remeshing during landscape evolution.

Water routing and the seasonal reservoir solver continue to calculate actual
water storage, levels, outlets and coverage. There is no copied lake mask or
artificial water addition. Refinement can still change shorelines where it
resolves existing slopes or sills; exact old lake area, level and IDs are not
invariants of the remap.

## River curves

Historical river bends were sampled in their old geographic coordinates after
the routing mesh changed. The renderer then snapped their first and last points
to new junctions, leaving interior samples on the old curve. Some inherited
endpoints in the reproduced refined world were over 30 km from their new routing
nodes, causing the visible teeth and severe width pinches.

The corrected sampling distributes the endpoint correction across each inherited
subcurve, attaching it to the refined routing reach while retaining its bend
variation and migration clock. Shared river junctions remain connected. Both
river readers apply the same correction to malformed courses in older snapshots
before normal lake and ocean anchoring. Ordinary saved courses whose endpoints
already match their routing nodes bypass this correction.

A second defect appeared where refinement introduced a very short routing edge
between long reaches. The display spline used the long neighbours' direction
and short handles, forcing a tight bend onto an originally straight 53 m reach.
Its curvature safety limit narrowed the river to a hairline. Unequal reaches now
share a tangent weighted toward the short reach; the long reaches absorb the
turn gradually. Handle lengths transition with the same weighting. Ordinary
length ratios keep their existing interpolation, with a smooth transition over
ratios of four to eight. This also corrects rare unequal joins in coarse meshes.
Actual tight meanders still retain their curvature limits.

Extreme unequal joins also exposed three narrow, concave river spans whose
fixed triangle diagonal produced an overlap. Those spans use the other diagonal
when both resulting triangles have valid winding. Convex spans, all sampled
positions, widths and animation attributes stay unchanged; the correction also
preserves geometry across packet boundaries.

## Verification

All **308 automated tests** pass, together with typecheck and formatting.

A controlled level-floor lake uses the actual remap, drainage solver and production
renderer. With identical 5,567-cell refined geometry and camera, the old remap
created 367 dry cells among 573 tested lake-interior cells and seven wet components.
The corrected remap leaves all 573 interior cells wet in one connected reservoir
and keeps a genuine 650 m island dry. Repeated refinement checks all four seasons,
storage against the new basin geometry, spill edges, routing balance and source
immutability. The largest reported relative water-ledger residual is below 6e-13.

Browser evidence is retained in `outputs/atlas-lake0-controlled-browser-qa.json`;
physical measurements are in
[`measurements/refinement-lake-preservation-2.18.2.json`](../../observations/measurements/refinement-lake-preservation-2.18.2.json).
The lake fixture is deliberately controlled and is not a reconstruction of the
user's full-resolution world.

River checks use an AETHER-6389 world with 10,242 original cells and 15,049 cells
after regional refinement. They cover saved-course repair, newly rebuilt courses,
downstream animation, packet continuations, and equivalent river triangles when
the same path is split across batches in globe and map projections.

The 53 m straight-reach regression includes both adjoining long reaches: its
minimum display-width cap rises from 5.64 m to over 3,172 m without shifting the
pinch onto a junction. In the reproduced view, samples capped below 100 m fall
from 129 to 30; none remains below 10 m. The remaining narrow bends are retained,
including a procedural meander whose analytical curvature supports its roughly
120 m cap. A thin bend is not necessarily a disconnected river.

Matched browser views pass on globe and flat map in Chromium with SwiftShader.
The coarse reference viewport remains pixel-identical in both projections;
the refined world keeps all 1,061 river paths, and flow animation advances
downstream. The two affected ribbon batches have no reversed triangles after
the concave-span correction. Camera and projection changes request no new
terrain samples or river assets. These are correctness checks, not hardware
performance measurements or a new maximum-resolution generation benchmark.
The [browser report](../../observations/measurements/refinement-browser-2.18.2.json) records fixture
sizes, matched-image results and the retained local screenshot artifacts.

Repository-wide lint still reports 12 existing `curly` errors in the unchanged
`TileDensity.ts` and `TileLod.ts`, plus four existing unused-variable warnings in
unrelated tests. Changed TypeScript files pass scoped lint.

## Existing worlds

Application version is 2.18.2; generator identity is 2.17.1. Existing 2.17.0
recipes remain accepted. Replaying a two-step refinement recipe preserves its
resolved mesh geometry while applying the corrected transfer.

Loading an already-refined snapshot preserves its recorded physical state. The
river display correction applies on reload without changing discharge, receivers
or saved geometry. Old terrain artifacts require regeneration from the exported
recipe to replay the saved refinement steps with the corrected terrain transfer,
or refining the original world again.
The renderer still uses fixed physical terrain detail and shared terrain/river
resources. Saving remains an explicit user action.
