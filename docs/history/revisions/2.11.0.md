# v2.11.0 — Artistic maps

Choose **Layers → Map style → Shaded relief** or **Ink atlas**, then switch between **Globe** and **Flat map**. Styles apply immediately to the generated world. Terrain exaggeration also works on the shaded relief map; Natural and Ink atlas keep their level map projection. Choosing a data layer restores its scientific palette, and choosing a map style returns to the overview.

Application version is 2.11.0; generator identity remains 2.10.0. No simulation module, saved project contract, deposit record, fertility value, terrain elevation, or water budget changes in this release. The existing 29 layers remain available alongside three overview styles, including Natural.

## Shaded relief

The style uses the existing indexed terrain surface with vegetation, rock, snow, and water pigments. Fragment derivatives recover each displayed face normal. Orienting that normal toward geographic up handles the map's reversed winding. Subtracting the broad globe normal makes local slope lighting readable across the visible hemisphere. A fixed upper-left light emphasizes slopes; steep shaded mountain faces receive an additional dark fold tint. This is directional cartographic shading, without cast shadows or ambient occlusion.

The flat layout extends the existing equirectangular projection. For the displayed spherical vertex radius `r`, base overlay lift `b`, and display multiplier `s = 4`:

```text
h = max(0, r - 1 - b) × s
x = longitude
y = latitude + 0.8 × h
z = b + h
```

Terrain exaggeration is already included in `r`; it remains a display parameter. Sea-level positions retain their geographic coordinates, while elevated land shifts northward and toward the viewer. This produces the oblique relief effect without tilting the entire map. The globe keeps its existing radial terrain exaggeration.

Longitude seam copies and pole triangles interpolate height together with their other attributes. Grid lines, optional coastlines, selection outlines, oxbows, and river vertices use the same height/shear transform. For map water vertices, `WaterSurface` finds the containing projected terrain face and interpolates its height barycentrically. River ribbons draw as a foreground cartographic network on the raised map so intervening facets do not break their courses; globe rivers retain terrain depth testing. Displayed river widths and height exaggeration are not physical measurements.

Picking on the raised map raycasts the actual terrain. Barycentric interpolation of the hit face's retained spherical positions recovers the geographic direction for cell selection, including duplicated seam triangles. The flat Natural/Ink atlas maps keep the existing plane picking path.

## Ink atlas

The surface uses ivory paper, muted gray-green water, soft vegetation washes, and dark ink. Seeded three-dimensional pigment noise follows spherical direction, so globe rotation and crossing the longitude seam do not move the texture. Pixel-footprint filtering fades grain that is too fine to resolve.

The same interpolated land/water mask used by Natural draws both ocean and lake shores. A narrow antialiased ink edge follows that mask, and broken parallel water-side bands suggest coastal hatching. There is no second shoreline mesh or detached triangular lake patch. Riverbanks and delta cells reuse their existing geographic masks with the atlas palette. Snow, ice, river activity, and habitat pigments continue to respond to the selected season.

### Mountain and forest symbols

`CartographicSymbols.ts` selects cells with a separate seed stream. Peaks and hills require elevation; tree types follow the forest biome and vegetation fields. Ocean and lake cells are excluded. Coastal distance, adjacent lake cells, and strong river channels keep symbols away from the main water boundaries. Symbols show terrain and vegetation categories, not additional generated mountains or counted trees.

Sampling targets approximately 18,000 candidate cells across resolutions and retains at most 12,000 symbols. One instanced mesh draws the selected symbols; map mode includes wrap copies at ±2π. The original anchors and variants stay fixed through season changes. Seasonal temperature changes their pigment and snow tint, not their locations.

The atlas contains four mountain/hill symbols and four forest symbols. Its generated RGB sheet has dark ink, warm ivory interiors, and a neutral background. Loading it creates two coverage channels before generating mipmaps:

```text
ink = 1 - smoothstep(0.22, 0.43, luminance)
paper = smoothstep(0.045, 0.10, red - blue)
      × smoothstep(0.55, 0.80, luminance)
```

Filtering these coverage channels preserves thin pen strokes while removing the neutral background. Applying the threshold after filtering the original RGB image instead caused fine symbols to disappear, which the render check caught. The shader mixes filtered ink and paper coverage with seasonal pigments.

Symbols are camera-facing quads with geographic anchors. They fade when smaller than a few screen pixels and near the globe horizon, and their maximum displayed width is bounded. This is an illustration layer rather than a perspective forest model. Dense symbols can overlap at some zoom levels; this release does not add label collision layout or terrain-specific custom artwork.

## Inspiration and assets

The shaded relief presentation is inspired by [Red Blob Games Mapgen4](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/mapgen4/), especially its [oblique projection explanation](https://simblob.blogspot.com/2018/09/mapgen4-oblique-projection.html) and [outline discussion](https://simblob.blogspot.com/2018/10/mapgen4-outlines.html). The implementation here is written for this prototype's spherical terrain and existing water geometry; it does not incorporate Mapgen4 source.

The user's attached pen-and-ink map informed the ivory, gray-green water, shore hatching, and grouped-symbol direction. `public/art/ink-symbol-atlas.png` is newly generated artwork for this project: a four-by-two sheet of four mountain/hill variants and four forest groups. Geographic placement and seasonal coloring are computed by the renderer.
