# Atlas 2.23.1 — mountain and forest coverage

The fantasy styles previously omitted large parts of broad mountain belts and
showed closed forest as scattered clumps. The placement rules now represent
the extent of these landforms, using larger overlapping peaks and denser canopy
illustrations. Classic parchment, Engraved explorer and Painted fantasy atlas
share the corrected geographic placements.

## Mountain belts

The previous detector required a sharp bilateral height drop within two degrees
at every step. Broad crests, flat high saddles and coastal profiles often failed
that test even when the surrounding land clearly formed a mountain range.

The detector now checks local and broader cross-sections, follows connected high
terrain between summits, and includes smaller peaks on broad high shoulders.
Finite mountain caps remain represented through their interiors. Featureless
plateaus and constant slopes still remain undecorated, and deep valleys and
water bodies separate ranges. Mountain silhouettes are larger at regional
scale and overlap along their paths.

## Forest canopy and shorelines

One source vertex per three-degree region previously won a forest illustration.
The new field samples forest class and vegetation inside physical triangles at
stable geographic locations about three-quarters of a degree apart. Closed
canopy keeps dense overlapping clusters; lower vegetation and transitional
woodland thin them deterministically. Actual forest on high or sloping ground
is no longer rejected just because the terrain could support a mountain.
Smaller canopy illustrations also remain visible at regional zoom levels.

Water protection previously marked an entire triangle's enclosing cap wet if
any corner contained even a tiny fraction of water. The replacement clips the
interpolated ocean and lake boundaries at half coverage, then conservatively
rasterizes their actual footprints. This preserves dry ground beside mixed
coastal triangles and partially covered lake cells.

Mountain ink is centered over the sampled crest. Its directional footprint is
fitted to dry ground, allowing width and height to shrink independently beside
a shore. A circular exclusion zone no longer erases an entire coastal range
just because its illustrations are tall.

## Rendering and compatibility

Placement remains geographic, independent of camera motion, source packet order
and duplicated triangle boundaries. The active style still uses one instanced
symbol draw and one symbol atlas. Completed placements remain cached at 49 bytes
per symbol and are reused between fantasy styles and projections.

Temporary typed selection fields occupy about 4.91 MiB regardless of world cell
count and are released after preparation. Denser illustrations increase the
instance and completed-placement buffers in proportion to their symbol count.
No disk cache or world-resolution recomputation is introduced.

The saved terrain, rivers, lakes and biome data are unchanged. Existing worlds
receive the new illustrations without regeneration. Application version is
2.23.1; generator identity remains 2.17.1. Saving remains manual.

## Verification

All 437 tests pass, alongside whole-project TypeScript and changed-file
formatting checks.

Focused regressions cover broad north–south and east–west mountain belts, high
caps, coastal profiles, lake-separated ranges, rainforest area coverage,
vegetation-dependent density, highland forest, interpolated mixed attributes,
refinement, packet order, water boundaries and placement-cache ownership.
An end-to-end coastal regression retains all 41 peaks that the circular
clearance rule reduced to two. Both projection footprints remain dry, including
near the poles and across the dateline.

Browser comparisons use identical terrain and camera settings before and after
the change. The AETHER-2408 fixtures recreate the reported seed; they are not the
user's exact saved snapshot, which was unavailable in this environment.
The 251,559-cell recreation reproduces the reported coastline, the two mountain
belts and the central rainforest; the screenshot's world contained 327,837 cells.

In the matching region (112°–78° W, 5° S–25° N), the share of eligible mountain
area within two degrees of a mountain illustration increased from 40.0% to
98.0%. Dense forest area within one degree of a tree cluster increased from
9.1% to 87.2%; for rainforest it increased from 7.4% to 93.2%. These are
area-weighted proximity checks on saved terrain cells, not percentages of map
pixels covered by ink. Mountain and forest illustrations deliberately exclude
one another where they overlap.

The same world produced 1,465 mountain marks and 2,990 forest clusters, compared
with 324 and 73 previously. One collector-only Node measurement took 2.07 s,
compared with 1.65 s before the change. Denser placement therefore adds initial
preparation work; the completed result continues to be reused between styles.
These timings do not measure browser frame rate or predict native GPU performance.

All six style/projection views use one instanced symbol draw and the same
completed placements. Camera and style/projection round trips reproduce
identical pixels, without additional terrain or river reads. Browser and WebGL
error checks pass; the matching region and lake shoreline views were inspected.
