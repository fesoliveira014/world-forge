# Atlas 2.21.0 — smooth tectonic plate regions

The tectonic plate layer now shows ownership on a height-independent surface:
a smooth sphere in Globe mode and a flat sheet in Flat map mode. The plate
colors and geographic borders remain the same. Relief remains available for
other layers, and returning to a raised layer restores the user's setting.

## Geometry and preparation

The tectonic reader gathers saved surface topology, cell-to-process transfers,
and process-grid plate IDs. It skips terrain heights, lake levels, climate,
seasonal records, and the spatial-record inverse lookup. Existing saved worlds
work without regeneration or new stored sidecars.

Each fixed packet simplifies connected same-plate interiors in three
deterministic passes. An interior vertex can collapse onto an adjacent saved
vertex only when the operation keeps the mesh manifold, preserves outward
winding, and respects a fixed one-degree limit on new edges. Every plate-border
vertex and packet-border vertex stays in place. Both semantic plate IDs and
colors constrain the operation, so unrelated plates cannot merge when palette
colors repeat. Colors remain flat across each triangle.

The angular limit retains globe curvature; it is independent of zoom and camera
position. Existing coarse edges are retained. Consequently small, coarse worlds
can keep their original triangle count, while dense worlds lose many redundant
interior triangles. The implementation retains spatial batches and their existing
hidden-face culling and GPU submission path.

Display heights are zero for the plate mesh and its grid, coastlines, borders,
rivers, and selection outline. Geographic sphere/map picking still inspects the
original cells and their actual elevations. Switching from a close plate view
to raised terrain moves the camera outside the restored terrain before drawing.

The physical manifest marks this layer as simplified. Its source triangle count
is an upper bound; renderer diagnostics report the actual prepared triangle count
separately. Other categorical layers retain their original terrain-following
meshes.

## Verification

- All 394 tests pass, alongside TypeScript, formatting, and changed-file lint.
- Geometry tests verify exact plate and packet edges, complete spherical area,
  original plate-color coverage, outward winding, semantic boundaries with
  repeated colors, deterministic output, and the bound on new edges.
- The tectonic reader reads only topology, transfer, and plate fields. Its packets
  are season-independent, and preparation does not change the saved world.
- Authentic 655,362-cell topology samples with synthetic plate IDs reduced eight
  12,288-triangle packets to 1,034–1,408 triangles. Full in-memory reader and
  simplifier time was approximately 36–40 ms per warmed packet. This excludes
  IndexedDB and graphics costs; browser measurements are separate.
- Coarse globe/map comparisons match the previous zero-relief images, including
  borders and the grid. Relief changes do not alter the new plate display.
- Browser inspection returns the original physical cell and height; switching
  back to Natural restores terrain relief and a safe camera distance.

This removes 93.6% of the plate triangles and approximately 90% of the GPU buffer
storage in that comparison. First opening is about 9% slower because region
simplification adds CPU preparation. The benefit is a much smaller resident mesh
and faster completed rendering; this version does not claim faster initial
loading. Measurements use Chromium with ANGLE/SwiftShader in the cloud and do
not establish hardware GPU frame rates. Display accounting is not whole-process
peak memory.

The coarse fixture has no triangle reduction under the fixed curvature limit and
matches the previous zero-relief images exactly. A locally refined 15,049-cell
fixture reduces 90,282 triangles to 76,436; its globe without overlays and map
with borders/grid match exactly. A small number of globe border pixels can differ
where depth-tested lines meet the changed interior facets. Plate-border geometry
itself remains fixed.

The maximum-world globe differs from the previous zero-relief image at 303 pixels:
296 silhouette/antialiasing pixels and seven isolated color-mix pixels. All are
within five pixels of an existing color or silhouette edge. The tested globe,
map, and polar interiors contain no background-colored holes. Camera, relief,
and projection changes reuse prepared packets without additional physical or
river reads.
