# Atlas 2.28.0 — lake salts and local soil salinity

Closed lakes can concentrate catchment salts instead of remaining only a
throughflow-based freshwater estimate. Lake releases carry dissolved salts into
downstream rivers and reservoirs. Salt affects local fertility and ordinary
vegetation potential without changing the climatic biome or the rain/PET budget.

## Controls and inspection

Land & water adds three generation settings:

- **Salt accumulation period:** an explicit representative hydrologic interval,
  initially 100 years, independent of tectonic age, erosion duration, reservoir
  initialization iterations, and refinement passes.
- **Catchment dissolved salts:** a runoff concentration, initially 0.05 g/L.
  Direct lake precipitation remains fresh.
- **Inherited marine salts:** an optional initial lake-water concentration,
  initially zero. This is an explicit scenario applied to initial lakes, not a
  historical reconstruction or an inference from oceanic crust.

Inspection reports modeled lake and river concentration in g/L, dissolved and
precipitated lake salt, salt age, and affected soil. Classification thresholds are
freshwater below 0.5 g/L, brackish below 5 g/L, saline below 50 g/L, and hypersaline
above that. Dry salt-bearing basins remain inspectable. These are generic model
categories; they do not establish drinking-water safety or crop suitability.

Old snapshots retain their stored values and explicitly unknown salinity.
Generate a new world to include the salt budget. Older recipes gain the new
defaults on regeneration, and existing resolved refinement plans remain
importable. Saving remains manual.

## Salt accounting

The salt pass runs once after final terrain, hydrology, refinement, and coarsening.
It uses the finalized receiver graph with virtual lake nodes. Evaporation has
already changed water storage and outflow; it never removes salt. The lake solve
integrates a well-mixed representative annual reservoir analytically:

`salt change = dissolved input − outflow × dissolved concentration`

The generic solubility cap is 260 g/L. Excess salt stays on the bed, including when
water storage is zero, and can dissolve if water returns. Upstream interval-average
salt exports supply downstream budgets, while contemporary outlet concentration
supplies the displayed river field. The solve has bounded work independent of
the chosen number of years. It does not reconstruct seasonal chemistry or the
transient travel time between a chain of lakes.

Sparse geographic salt parcels preserve the original resolved salt footprint
across later mesh changes. They preserve mass and position when refining; changed
water-body ownership can partition the same inventory among newly resolved lakes
or exposed ground. Refinement does not introduce another accumulation interval or
marine starting load. Subcell salt chemistry is not newly invented by adding
cells. A per-cell inventory supports inspection and the existing conservative
terrain transfer, while parcel ownership prevents repeated coarse-cell averaging
from mixing adjacent lakes.

The world salt ledger records initial, weathering, exported, and retained
kilograms, its residual, and the actual years advanced by the salt pass.

## Soil and ecology

Shore fringes, seasonal lake drawdown, exposed salt beds, and physical saline river
bank/flood footprints provide a separate soil-contact estimate. Its concentration
and affected fraction reduce fertility and vegetation in proportion to affected
exposed land. This is a local porewater-exposure proxy, not an independent soil
salt inventory or a groundwater simulation.

Fresh inflowing channels retain their own water quality and bank habitat benefit.
Saline releases lose that freshwater benefit. Wet saline ground keeps its wet
footprint without automatically becoming freshwater swamp or peatland. Climate
biomes, aridity, terrain, runoff, and lake water balance remain independent of
these ecological salt effects.

## Validation

Focused tests cover analytic flushing, evaporation concentration, precipitation
and redissolution, downstream transport, salt-ledger closure, geographic remapping,
refinement without aging, legacy snapshots, controls, paged inspection, local soil
stress, and fresh inflows beside saline water.

`scripts/measure-salinity.ts` generates AETHER-2408 at 40,962 terrain and weather
cells, writes a numerical report, and optionally exports an ordinary snapshot
fixture for browser inspection. See
[`measurements/salinity-2.28.0.json`](../../observations/measurements/salinity-2.28.0.json).

All 532 tests pass, together with TypeScript, changed-file formatting, and the
production build. Browser inspection uses Chromium with ANGLE/SwiftShader and
the production view/inspector components. AETHER-2408's 365 reservoirs include
218 fresh, 8 brackish, 14 saline, 45 hypersaline, and 80 dry basins under the stated
defaults. The salt-ledger residual is approximately 2.6 × 10⁻¹⁵ of the supplied
salt. This is a model result, not calibration to observed water chemistry.

Application and generator versions are 2.28.0.
