# v2.3.1: desert classification and vegetation cover

## What the saved world showed

The v2.3 riverbank change addressed local water access. It did not address the broader classification and shading bias. Using AETHER-25711's retained climate and hydrology, about 22% of land was classified as desert. Another 18.7% was non-desert vegetation with less than 20% modeled cover, before the renderer reduced its visible greenery further. These are area-weighted model measurements, not screenshot measurements.

Three rules contributed:

1. Desert began at `PET / rain > 2.3`, equivalent to `rain / PET < 0.435`, or at an absolute rainfall cutoff of 150 mm. This grouped much semi-arid terrain into desert and ignored low evaporative demand in cool climates.
2. Vegetation multiplied `(1 - aridity * 0.3)` by `rain / 1200`, penalizing limited rainfall twice. Annual-mean temperature then suppressed cover in places with cold winters but usable summers.
3. The natural shader applied another aridity penalty to the resulting cover, making even classified grassland look mostly sandy.

## Changes

- Desert now requires `PET / rain > 5`, corresponding to `rain / PET < 0.2`. Temperature and seasonal rainfall still distinguish the remaining biomes; semi-arid land can become grassland or savanna. There is no target desert percentage or rainfall multiplier.
- Background vegetation potential uses `waterAvailability = clamp(actualET / max(1, PET))`, which comes from the existing seasonal soil and snow budget. Cover is `(1 - exp(-3 * waterAvailability)) * clamp((warmestSeason - 1) / 14)`. The response curve is an explicit worldbuilding approximation, not a measured global vegetation-cover law.
- Riverbank access still raises cover locally. Ocean, lakes, and permanent ice remain bare. A warm cell with no available water cannot gain vegetation merely from warmth.
- The natural shader blends sand and grass directly using this cover. Forest canopy also respects cover. Grass pigment is slightly greener; procedural detail, mountain shading, water, and snow retain their existing rendering.
- The aridity overlay now displays the full 0–6 PET/rain range instead of saturating at 3. The inspector's vegetation potential uses the revised ecology field.

The aridity boundary follows the common climate-index distinction between arid conditions below P/PET 0.2 and semi-arid conditions from 0.2 to 0.5. Mapping those climate classes to this generator's few biome categories is a modeling choice; climate classes do not uniquely determine real vegetation. Reference: [Zomer, Xu, and Trabucco (2022), Global Aridity Index and Potential Evapotranspiration Database](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01493-1).

## AETHER-25711 comparison

The v2.3.0 and v2.3.1 ecology functions were run on the same retained 655,362-cell world, including the v2.3 selected rivers. No weather rerun was needed because these changes occur after climate and drainage.

| Area-weighted measure | v2.3.0 | v2.3.1 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Land classified as desert | 21.96% | 0.32% |
| Mean land vegetation potential | 14.14% | 51.41% |
| Mean potential on cells now classified as grassland/savanna | 8.78% | 65.90% |

Of total land area, 15.65 percentage points changed from desert to grassland and 5.98 to savanna. The very small remaining desert fraction is an outcome of this seed's existing rainfall and PET under the revised classification, not a promised Earth-like global distribution. The simplified climate/PET model is not calibrated to reproduce Earth's biome proportions. Adjusting the global climate to hit an arbitrary percentage would conceal that distinction.

The revised ecology pass took about 0.12 seconds in this environment. It adds no weather iterations or retained arrays, and it does not change water fluxes, river selection, reservoir levels, or terrain.
