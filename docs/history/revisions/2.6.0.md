# v2.6.0 — volcanic construction and landscape aging

This release addresses AETHER-94633: untouched intraplate margins, oversized ocean islands, widespread persistent depressions, and tropical dryland classification. It keeps the TypeScript prototype's flat arrays, one generation worker, explicit stage order, and single final climate/hydrology feedback. C3 remains the intended native target.

## Observable behavior

- Land inside a stationary plate can change through waves, weathering, sediment delivery, and intraplate volcanism. Initial crust fields remain available for comparison.
- Volcanic relief must first build up from the seabed. A submerged edifice does not suddenly acquire its entire construction height as above-water elevation.
- Sources along convergent ocean margins and divergent faults depend on plate motion. Intraplate source positions and eruption windows are seeded, independent of the requested geological end date.
- Shallow old depressions can fill with sediment or acquire an eroded outlet. Remaining lakes use the same annual balance and real spill rules as before. No lake visibility cutoff or lake-count quota is added.
- Final rivers are rerouted on the modified terrain. Closed basins still terminate their rivers; spilling lakes still pass only actual outflow downstream.
- Dry tropical conditions are evaluated before savanna assignment, using temperature and rainfall seasonality as well as PET/rain. Rivers still support local banks without converting an entire desert cell to forest.
- Zero erosion disables landscape aging; zero volcanic construction disables geological emplacement; zero hotspots retains only fault sources. Zero geological duration skips accumulated volcanism and long-term aging, retaining the static terrain path and its small river pass.

## 1. Building volcanic terrain

`volcanism.ts` creates `hotspotCount` stationary mantle-frame sources using a separate seed suffix. Each has a unit position, a finite eruption interval, a 110–200 km influence radius, and an effective construction rate of 220–440 m/Myr. The fixed schedule spans the supported geological interval; it is not regenerated at each step or rescaled to the requested end time.

During each existing crust step, `crust-evolution.ts` transports the previous volcanic relief through the inverse incremental plate rotation. It accepts it only when the nearest source belongs to the current receiving plate. Opened ocean can carry new deposits even though it has no surviving reference basement. Old volcanic relief relaxes by `exp(-dt / 220 Myr)`, a combined empirical loading/aging proxy rather than a conserved crust-volume ledger.

Fault sources are deterministic given the seed, ownership, and prescribed plate velocities:

- Convergence with at least one oceanic side places a source on the overriding side. Continental collision contributes mechanical relief instead of a second arc.
- Divergence supplies decompression sources on both sides, at a lower rate.
- Transform motion alone is not assumed to generate magma.

A heap propagates fault influence over `130 km × tectonicWidth`, constrained to the same plate. Stronger sources dominate overlap rather than accumulating one volcano per boundary edge. Hotspots add Gaussian influence using local chord distance. Sources are integrated with a saturating construction law:

```text
carried = interpolate(previousVolcanic, inverseIncrement × position)
          × exp(-dt / 220 Myr)
rate = volcanicActivity × (faultRate + sum(activeHotspotRates))
newVolcanic = carried + (10,000 m - carried)
                        × (1 - exp(-rate × dt / 10,000 m))
```

`volcanicAge` increments with the carried material and resets where emplacement exceeds 5 m/Myr. Young large deposits resist the separate long-term erosion pass. These arrays describe accumulated construction and a recent-emplacement proxy, not lava stratigraphy, lithology, eruption hazards, or irreversible mineral provenance.

## 2. Correct ocean-island elevation

The earlier calculation included uplift when deciding whether a raw sample was land, then gave newly emergent samples an additional full mountain term. Present collision and arc families could also stack. This amplified some ocean islands and produced the reported 14,085 m peak.

The new calculation composes an actual base with relief:

```text
base = reconstructedBasement + materialResidual
basementHeight = base > 0 ? lowlandProfile(base) + valleyDetail : 0.55 × base
mechanical = max(uplift, 0) × mountainTexture + min(uplift, 0)
elevation = basementHeight + mechanical + volcanicConstruction
finalHeight = elevation > 0 ? elevation × reliefScale : elevation
```

Positive present-day feature families use their maximum rather than their sum; negative tectonic contributions remain additive. With geological history enabled, arc and ridge magmatic terms are supplied by accumulated volcanic construction instead of being added again as instantaneous features.

High volcanic islands are still possible. The change removes the elevation discontinuity and duplicate construction, rather than imposing a universal island-height cap.

## 3. Aging inherited relief

Independent 100–200 km valley noise previously remained at full strength forever, including on broad old plains. It now attenuates with geological duration and erosion:

```text
maturity = durationMyr × erosion × (1 - 0.85 × youth)
inheritedReliefFactor = 0.2 + 0.8 × exp(-maturity / 65 Myr)
```

`youth` combines active uplift, recent substantial volcanic construction, and a high-latitude preservation proxy. This is a terrain-construction parameterization. It is not an additional sediment flux and does not imply that a glacier simulation has run. Some inherited relief is retained to avoid featureless plains and preserve drainage gradients.

## 4. Rain, slope weathering, wind, and waves

`landscape.ts` reuses the provisional four-season climate, restricting the surface to the process grid. It does not run weather for every geological step or reconstruct past climate. Rock resistance is a seeded material-frame field at a physical scale around 180 km.

Rain and slope weathering use effective diffusivities of 12–60 million m²/Myr before erosion, rock-resistance, and age modifiers. These are regional detachment coefficients, not measured small-slope soil diffusivities. Wind adds much weaker deflation where precipitation is small relative to evaporative demand. Rain-dependent background weathering acts on exposed relief.

For a cell `i`, slope detachment sums downhill contributions:

```text
rate_i = sum_j[D_i × (sharedEdgeLength / centreDistance)
                  × max(height_i - height_j, 0)] / area_i
```

Coastal edges use a wave term controlled by wind speed, rock resistance, and Coastal erosion. The lower reference is a -30 m abrasion platform, not the abyssal seabed. A cell must become ocean-connected before it exposes the next shoreline. This makes old headlands and narrow coastal ground retreat even within a plate interior. The current proxy uses wind speed, not a resolved wave spectrum, tides, or directional fetch model.

The explicit timestep is bounded by `0.35 × cellArea / summedConductance`. Time and widths are physical; increasing surface detail does not multiply the number of coarse landscape steps. The final correction is interpolated to fine land cells. Removed loose sediment is consumed before bedrock, and detached volume is computed using actual fine-cell areas. Enclosed interiors are prevented from eroding below the datum.

## 5. Basin infill and outlet erosion

Two complementary mechanisms are required. The first full-resolution trial showed that infill alone left too much lake area; adding a lake display threshold would have reintroduced the previously fixed disappearing-water problem.

`sediment.ts` uses a temporary cell priority flood solely to deliver geological sediment. It is not a second river or water receiver forest. Connected depressions with a common escape height accumulate upstream detached material. A sorted height/area curve raises the floor from the bottom upward, limited by available material and the escape sill. Lake persistence controls the effective fraction retained as basin infill; the remainder represents bypass/unresolved downstream sediment export. Excess sediment is accounted for as export.

`outlet-erosion.ts` uses `cornerFlood`, the same geometric routine used by `drainage.ts`. For each flooded patch it finds the floor and escape anchor. A climate-, resistance-, duration-, and persistence-dependent incision budget determines how far its sill may lower. A shallow basin can get a complete escape; a deeper basin can retain a reduced water-storage depression.

Before editing, it follows a complete parent path to lower ground or the ocean. The path must fit a 750 km length bound and the allowed cut depth, including the actual incident-cell elevations. It cuts all three incident cells where needed so the reconstructed corner surface genuinely opens. A tiny descending grade avoids replacing a lake with an exactly flat channel. Rejected paths leave the basin unchanged. Excavated volume is accounted for as unresolved downstream sediment export; grain classes and explicit coastal deposition are not modeled.

Final `routeWater` recomputes heights, priority flood, receivers, reservoirs, discharge, headwaters, and river-system metadata from the changed terrain. This preserves real river destinations and avoids treating an erosion-only path as authoritative water routing.

## 6. Dry tropical biomes

The old sole desert condition, `PET / rain > 5`, missed warm dry environments under the simplified PET estimate. The classifier now also applies a Köppen-style seasonal dry-climate boundary before savanna selection:

```text
seasonalOffset = warm-half share >= 0.70 ? 280 mm
               : warm-half share <= 0.30 ?   0 mm
               :                          140 mm
desertRainBoundary = max(0, 20 × annualTemperature + seasonalOffset) / 2
```

The warm-half estimate combines the warmest representative season with half of each neighboring season. Polar/ice rules still precede this test; the existing very-high-aridity condition remains sufficient. This is a four-season ecological approximation, not full monthly Köppen-Geiger classification. It does not alter rainfall to achieve a biome quota, nor does it remove riverbank vegetation.

## Controls, fields, and C3 mapping

| Control | Default | Meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Volcanic construction | 1× | Geological emplacement rate; range 0–2. |
| Intraplate hotspots | 8 | Seeded source count; range 0–24. |
| Coastal erosion | 1× | Wave contribution; range 0–2. |
| Wind erosion | 1× | Dry-ground deflation contribution; range 0–2. |
| Lake persistence | 0.35 | Higher values preserve more depression capacity; range 0–1. |

There are 23 visual layers. Volcanic construction reads `CrustHistory.volcanic`; Erosion & deposition reads `Terrain.surfaceChange`. The inspector exposes both. The model retains the initial crust map. Old v2.5 project files receive the new defaults on import.

The additional hot loops use flat scalar arrays, local adjacency, matrix transforms, and binary heaps. A C3 port can retain these structures directly. The temporary sediment flood and volcano source list do not require a generic simulation scheduler, dynamic per-cell objects, a mantle solver, or a second persistent hydrology graph.

## Verification and measurements

The exact AETHER-94633 comparison uses the attached v2.5 project definition: 655,362 surface cells, 40,962 process cells, 200 Myr, 4 cm/year plate speed, and erosion 0.7. New fields take v2.6 defaults. Final measurements follow. Percentages use non-ocean area, including lake surfaces; they are not Earth target proportions.

The focused suite covers seamount-to-island emergence, seeded source schedules, intraplate coastal retreat, sediment inventory, real outlet incision, retained deeper basins, and the existing geometry/water/tributary/lake contracts. No broad seed sweep or browser screenshot suite is required. Maximum weather resolution retains its existing two-climate-pass cost.

## References and limits

Hotspot tracks are informed by [USGS: Hotspots](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/hotspots.html). Coastal erosion depends on waves, exposure, and substrate; see [USGS: Coastal Erosion](https://www.usgs.gov/node/26771). Seasonal dry-climate classification follows the general approach documented by the [Chalmers Climate Dynamics Group](https://hanschen.org/koppen), adapted to four seasons and the prototype's PET rule.

The model does not simulate mantle forces, irreversible subduction/welding, lava-flow routing, dated stratigraphy, changing sea level, glacial excavation, detailed wave fetch, or historical climate. Unmodified coast segments can still remain smooth; tectonic motion alone should not distort a rigid plate interior. New shoreline variety comes from localized construction and erosion. The improvement target is a coherent, inspectable worldbuilding model with bounded cost, not a geoscience reconstruction of Earth.
