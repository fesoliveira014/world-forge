# v2.4: coastal catchments and inland basins

## Observed problems

Reproduced the supplied AETHER-43246 v2.3.1 project at 655,362 surface cells and 40,962 climate cells. The two inspected cells matched the screenshots: cell 180,093 was at −1,404.38 m, and cell 317,727 at −2,008.07 m. Both were dry land. The lowest inland lake level was −2,498.42 m; total non-ocean area below −1 km was about 6.63 million km².

The cause was in terrain construction. All negative heights received the ocean-floor profile, but only the largest connected negative region was marked ocean. Other enclosed regions retained oceanic depths while being passed to the land budget and closed-lake solver. The generator did not simulate a tectonic isolation or desiccation history for those regions.

The coastal pattern had a separate terrain cause. Continental heights are deliberately compressed to produce broad plains. The small inherited terrain variations were compressed too, leaving slopes that ran almost uniformly for hundreds of kilometres. Steepest-downhill drainage could then produce many long, nearly parallel channels. Merely hiding more headwaters would not add shared valleys.

## Terrain changes

`terrainNoise` now supplies an independent, continuous lowland-relief field. Two noise bands use physical lattice scales of 200 and 100 km. Their contribution is applied after the lowland height compression:

```ts
const valleys = Math.min(140, plains * 0.65) * noise.lowlandRelief;
const landHeight = (plains + valleys + mountains) * config.reliefScale;
```

The amplitude is bounded and fades with the available lowland height near the shoreline. This adds low valleys and drainage divides without turning plains into mountain ranges or changing the initial sign of the continent field. River receivers still follow the resulting terrain; no cross-divide river connections are invented.

After identifying the ocean, `shapeContinentalBasins` finds each depression's lowest escape sill with a priority flood. A depression containing negative terrain is then fitted **above sea level and below that sill**, before climate and drainage. For original height `h` below positive sill elevation `s`:

```ts
const continentalHeight = (s * s) / (2 * s - h);
```

This profile is positive and monotone, matching the original height and slope at the sill. The surrounding terrain supplies its scale; there is no fixed negative-depth cap. It reshapes the entire affected depression, including its positive slopes, so the original zero contour does not become an artificial ridge. The exceptional zero-height sill produces a sea-level floor. Ocean bathymetry, coastlines, surrounding ridges, and ordinary positive basins keep their original heights. Bedrock and surface height are updated together.

This is a default terrain-construction rule, not a lake-water fill or sediment-conservation simulation. The height-only flood is temporary and costs O(cells × log cells); it does not create another river receiver graph. Climate and water balance then determine whether a basin contains a lake or stays dry. Later erosion still follows its existing volume budget.

Kilometre-scale drawdowns are not geologically impossible: research on the Messinian Mediterranean discusses such exceptional conditions, with uncertainty in their timing and amplitude. That does not establish such a history for this generated world. See [García-Castellanos et al. (2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12248288/).

## River information for later settlement modeling

`Rivers` now exposes two additional arrays on the existing corner graph:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `system[c]` | Shared terminal ocean-mouth corner or closed-lake node. Upstream catchments retain the same system through actual reservoir spills. |
| `streamOrder[c]` | Strahler order on selected channels: headwaters begin at 1; equal-order tributaries raise downstream order; smaller tributaries do not. Zero off-channel. |

These are calculated while visiting the existing temporary lake-aware accumulation graph. It still derives from `drainage.receiver`; there is no second authoritative routing forest. A reverse topological pass supplies outlet identity. Two retained arrays add five bytes per corner; temporary work stays linear in corners and lakes.

The inspector displays system ID and stream order. The river-basin layer colors catchments by the shared terminal outlet, using each cell's largest-discharge corner to resolve mixed catchments. Spilling lakes connect river systems; closed lakes end them. Separate coastal mouths remain separate even when they are geographically close.

For later settlement logic, these fields can be combined with existing discharge, upstream catchment area, bank vegetation/access, slope, and climate. They do not constitute a settlement suitability score, navigability model, or proof of perennial fresh water. System IDs identify one generated snapshot, not persistent civilization identities across regeneration.

## Focused verification

The supplied project is checked through a complete generation, including both weather passes and erosion. Coastal systems here mean channels with at least 20 m³/s within 500 km of an ocean coast, whose outlet lies between 60°S and 60°N. An unbranched system has maximum selected-channel Strahler order 1 in that measured region. These counts describe the defined sample, not every blue pixel or only the screenshot crop.

The eight core tests cover the existing water/lake contracts, ocean depth retention, positive basin floors with retained slopes and sills, and connected versus closed-lake system membership and tributary order. Browser visual QA was not requested and was not performed.

Older project JSON files remain loadable. Reload the Site and load the supplied JSON to regenerate with v2.4.0; terrain and hydrology can change even with the same seed because the terrain model changed.
