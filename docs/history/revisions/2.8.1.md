# v2.8.1 — broad alluvial river corridors

The previous model mostly represented immediate banks. Its persistent width was capped at 4 km per side, while seasonal inundation used a short channel-transit estimate. This understated the broad productive land around major lowland rivers.

## Changes

- `floodplainWidths` separates persistent alluvial habitat from seasonal inundation. Qualifying river supply, longitudinal slope, and deposited sediment set the habitat width. High banks and the resolved space beside the channel constrain both belts.
- Inundation uses a bounded fraction of overbank flow retained for an estimated floodplain drainage time, capped at 14 days. Flat valleys drain more slowly than steep reaches. No seasonal excess means no modeled inundation, while a large perennial river can still support an alluvial habitat belt.
- The new **Land & water → Floodplain extent** control ranges from 0.5–2×. It adjusts habitat width and spreads the same flood-volume estimate more shallowly, without changing discharge or adding water. Terrain limits remain in force.
- The rendered corridor retains its full vegetation enhancement across the first 30% of its width before fading outward. Cell-average vegetation uses the same profile. Natural world, Biomes, Vegetation, and Floodplains share these widths in both layouts.
- Regional biome labels, atmospheric rainfall, river destinations, and reservoir budgets are unchanged. Old project definitions receive the new control's default of 1× when loaded.

## Interpretation

These are uncalibrated worldbuilding parameters, not a flood-frequency model or measurements of Mesopotamia. The persistent belt approximates access to an alluvial landscape; it does not solve groundwater flow, sediment nutrients, or crop yields. Width remains limited to the two resolved cells adjacent to each reach, so very broad plains can still be constrained at high surface resolution.

[NASA's Mesopotamian marsh comparison](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/world-of-change/mesopotamia-marshes/) shows both natural wetland vegetation and irrigated farmland. Irrigation and canals belong to the later civilization simulation. [USGS valley geomorphology](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sir/2010/5016/section5.html) motivates the distinction between broad valley floors and confined reaches; the coefficients here are prototype choices.
