# Atlas 2.25.0 — automatic final feature refinement

Automatic feature detail runs at the end of new-world generation, after the
last landscape and climate/water calculations. It examines the finished physical
fields and adds local cells where the available resolution leaves features
poorly represented. It runs independently of the earlier adaptive terrain passes.

## Feature selection

The reusable `FeatureDetector` interface emits a source cell, importance score,
and optional target spacing. Four initial detectors identify:

- Partly flooded lake shores, wet/dry boundaries and small lake footprints.
- River corridors, junctions, bends and flowing lake outlets.
- Land cells beside the ocean.
- Sharp changes in terrain slope and curvature.

Lake mixing is evaluated separately in each season. A lake interior that is
completely flooded in one season and dry in another is not treated as a mixed
shoreline simply because its annual average is partly wet. Detectors use physical
fields rather than display colors or biome labels.

Candidates merge deterministically, keeping their contributing detector IDs.
The selector reserves half of the estimated work budget equally among active
detectors, ranked by each detector’s own scores, then spends the remaining budget
by overall importance. This prevents abundant lake outlets from excluding
independent coastline or terrain features. Immediate neighbors already covered
by a chosen region are skipped. Selected regions have graded spacing transitions.
New detectors can join the registry without changing remeshing, generation,
configuration, or recipe replay.

## Limits and controls

Under **World parameters → Automatic feature detail**:

- **Final detail passes:** zero, one or two. Fresh worlds start with one.
- **Feature target spacing:** desired spacing, initially 10 km. Each pass moves
  at most halfway from the local spacing toward finer detail.
- **Extra cell budget:** maximum added cells across all final passes, initially
  20% of the surface cell count immediately before those passes.

The planner estimates work including transition regions. Estimates are
conservative, so the result may use fewer cells than the budget permits. The mesh
inserter separately enforces the exact insertion limit and always finishes a
valid triangulation before stopping. Very small budgets may be insufficient to fit a region from every detector. The target is a goal
for selected regions, not a guaranteed global resolution.

Quick preview disables the final pass; Balanced enables it. Old recipes missing
these fields receive zero passes, preserving their geometry. Newly generated
worlds include resolved final-pass plans, separate from initial and regional
refinement plans, so exported recipes reproduce the selected regions and limits.

## Terrain and water

Refinement remaps the existing total terrain height and sediment conservatively,
then rebuilds transfers, land water, drainage and river channels. Climate forcing
stays fixed. No additional geological or erosion time passes. River courses,
deltas, ecology and resources are prepared after the final mesh is settled.

The pass does not invent independent high-frequency bedrock terrain. It gives
existing terrain and water features more cells, allowing shorelines and drainage
to be resolved more precisely. Current saved worlds are not silently changed,
and manual regional refinement does not trigger another global feature pass.
Saving remains manual. Saved-world configuration is normalized on both startup
and Library opens, so legacy worlds keep final refinement off without missing
control values.

Application and generator versions are 2.25.0. Legacy generator recipes remain
supported; their original signatures are normalized only for the three new
configuration defaults.

## Verification

All 472 tests and whole-project TypeScript checks pass.

Focused tests cover feature detection, deterministic selection, cell budgets,
multiple detectors, replay, async generation parity and compatibility with
regional refinement. A controlled inland sea keeps its connected wet interior,
exposed island, original terrain height and seasonal water accounting.

The recreated AETHER-2408 Balanced cloud fixture grows from 251,559 to 267,301
cells: 15,742 additions, within its 50,311-cell limit. The 591 selected regions
include 96 coastline, 348 lake-shore, 420 river and 310 relief matches (categories
overlap). An independent check finds 204 selected regions outside the original
lake-outlet cells. Paired median nearest-neighbor spacing at selected lake shores
falls from 37.208 km to 18.358 km.

Retained array buffers grow from 228.08 to 240.69 MiB. Final feature stages take
8.047 seconds in the synchronous cloud reference run. Whole generation timings
are single observations, affected by concurrent checks; they are not a promised
browser runtime. The planner conservatively uses 31.3% of its allowed insertion
budget in this fixture. It does not promise to refine every unresolved feature
or reach 10 km in one pass.

See [the measured comparison](../../observations/measurements/final-feature-refinement-2.25.0.json)
and rerun it with `node --import tsx scripts/measure-feature-refinement.ts`.
The fixture reproduces the seed and reference settings, not the user's exact
saved world.

Browser checks verify the native controls, legacy saved-world defaults, enabling
a pass, and reader-worker recipe export. The measured world renders in globe and
flat-map projections without browser or WebGL errors. Comparison captures wait
for all enabled terrain, grid and river uploads to finish. See the
[browser report](../../observations/measurements/final-feature-browser-2.25.0.json).
